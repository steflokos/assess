# Over-the-air update system

> **Disclaimer.** This example was generated by AI. The data is made up. It does not describe a real system, and it is not a model of how a normal system looks. Do not use it in production or as the basis of a real assessment. Use it only to see how assess works.

A threat analysis and risk assessment (TARA) of an over-the-air software update system, written as files for
[assess](../../README.md). It shows what a finished assessment looks like. Replace the data with your own.

## Try it

```sh
assess validate -strict .                 # check the files against the schema
assess eval .                             # feasibility and risk of every threat scenario
assess explain update-package-disclosure .   # how one value was calculated, step by step
assess lint -strict .                     # the rules in rules/
assess open .                             # what is still open: nothing
assess report .                           # out/report.html, the whole assessment as one page
assess diagram .                          # out/architecture.svg
```

## What is in it

- `item/` is the item: the functions, the architecture (components, interfaces, data and channels) and the assumptions.
- `tara/` holds the assets, the damage scenarios, the threat scenarios, the attack steps and the controls.
- `concept/risks.yaml` holds the risks with the treatment decisions: avoid, reduce, share or accept, each with a reason.
- `rules/basic.yaml` holds the rules that `assess lint` checks. Two of them are rules of the project's own process:
  every goal is tracked, and a shared risk names its agreement.
- `policy.yaml` holds the numbers the calculation uses, among them how far scalability raises the impact. `project.yaml` holds the information for the report.
- `iterations.yaml` holds the two closed iterations of the treatment decisions, with their hashes. Run `assess verify .` to
  check it. It is not signed. `assess sign` adds your signature, and the README of assess explains how.
